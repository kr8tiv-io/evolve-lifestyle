EVOLVE ← The Journal Stays · October 31, 2026 · 14 min read The 10 Best Places to Stay in Western Canada With a Breathtaking View From Your Room From a helicopter-only Rockies lodge to glass chalets under the Yukon aurora, here are ten Western Canada stays where the view is the room, with prices and how to get there. Some trips are about the hike, the paddle or the drive. Others are about one moment: you pull back the curtain with a coffee in your hand, and a glacier, a turquoise lake, a wild Pacific beach or a sky full of green aurora is sitting right there. Western Canada has more of those moments than almost anywhere on earth. We went looking for the stays where the view isn't something you drive to. It's the thing you wake up to. Some of these places are famous hotels you can drive right up to. Others need a helicopter, a floatplane or a boat. All of them were checked against their official websites in late October 2026, and every price below comes from a named source with the date we checked it. Prices change often, so confirm with the property before you plan around a number. Here are the best places to stay in Western Canada with a view, from the Rockies to the Pacific to the North. For each one, we tell you which room type actually has the view, because at most of these places, not every room does. Mount Assiniboine Lodge, British Columbia Photo: Mount Assiniboine Lodge Why the view is worth it Mount Assiniboine rises to 3,618 m (per BC Parks), and its popular nickname is the "Matterhorn of the Rockies." The lodge sits in Mount Assiniboine Provincial Park, near Lake Magog, with that pyramid of a peak right in front of you. Ask for a room or cabin facing the mountain, because not every room does. There are no roads in, so the only noise is wind and the odd marmot. Getting there You either hike or ski in (the classic route from the Mount Shark trailhead is a long day) or fly by helicopter. Flights for 2027 are listed at $265 per person one way from Mount Shark and $299 from Canmore, plus tax. In summer, flights run Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, and your stay has to line up with those days even if you walk. Best season Summer runs late June to early October, and a winter ski season runs March. The lodge lists summer/fall 2027 as June 20 to October 1, 2027. Price For summer 2027, a private lodge room or a cabin for 3 to 5 people is $539 per person per night plus 6.2% tax, with all meals, afternoon tea and guided hikes included. A two-person private cabin is $659 per person. Heads-up: the lodge lists both summer and winter 2027 as sold out, with a summer waitlist opening December 15, 2026. If you hike in from Mount Shark, our Alberta and BC hiking guide and lightweight packing kit will help you carry less. Book: assiniboinelodge.com 2. Lake O'Hara Lodge, British Columbia Photo: Lake O'Hara Lodge Why the view is worth it Lake O'Hara in Yoho National Park is one of the most protected corners of the Rockies. Road access is limited, so the Lake Louise crowds just aren't there. Book a Lakeshore Cabin: they sit right on the water, and the lake turns from jade to deep blue as the light moves across the peaks. Getting there The lodge bus leaves from the Lake O'Hara parking lot on the Trans-Canada Highway, 12 km west of Lake Louise and 13 km east of Field, and climbs an 11 km road that's closed to private cars and bikes. Lodge guests ride free as part of the rate. Day visitors need a seat on the Parks Canada bus, which is handed out by random draw each spring. In 2026, the bus season ran June 19 to October 4. Best season Summer and early fall, for hiking and the golden larches. The lodge also runs a winter season, when you ski or snowshoe in. Price The lodge lists Lakeshore Cabins at $1,495 per night for two guests. That includes meals, afternoon tea, the round-trip bus, taxes and gratuities, with a three-night minimum. Can't get a room? Day visitors can still reach the lake by the Parks Canada bus. See how in our list of Canada's most breathtaking views . Book: lakeohara.com 3. Moraine Lake Lodge, Alberta Photo: Moraine Lake Lodge Why the view is worth it Moraine Lake might be the most photographed lake in Canada, with its vivid blue water under the Valley of the Ten Peaks. According to the lodge, every room looks out on the lake. Overnight guests get it at sunrise and dusk, after the day crowds have gone. Getting there The lodge is about a 20-minute drive from the village of Lake Louise in Banff National Park. Moraine Lake Road is closed to personal vehicles, but registered lodge guests can drive up and park, and the lodge sends access details 30 days before arrival. Day visitors have to use the Parks Canada shuttle, Roam Transit or a commercial bus. Best season It's open in summer only. The lodge's FAQ lists the season as June 1 to October 1. Price The official rate table only shows prices once you pick dates. Third-party site Scenic and Savvy estimates about $1,300 to $1,900 per night before tax for 2026, with breakfast, canoeing and guided hikes included. Treat that as an estimate. Book: morainelake.com 4. Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise, Alberta Photo: Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise Why the view is worth it This is the easiest world-class view on the list. Book a Lakeview room and you look straight down Lake Louise to the Victoria Glacier. In winter the lake freezes into a skating rink with the glacier behind it. Getting there Drive about 2 hours west of Calgary on the Trans-Canada Highway into Banff National Park. You'll need a Parks Canada pass. Best season It's open year-round. Summer is the classic turquoise lake, and winter brings skating and the ice castle crowds. Price Fairmont's room page lists the Fairmont Lakeview King as 230 sq ft with lake and Victoria Glacier views. Third-party rate tracker Fermata estimates typical Lakeview King rates at around $1,019 per night, from about $682 in January to $2,062 in June. These are estimates, so check live rates on Fairmont's site. Only the Lakeview categories have the view, so don't book a standard room. Book: fairmont.com/lake-louise 5.
